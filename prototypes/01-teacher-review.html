1. OPERATING MECHANISM

A transformer is a static machine used to transfer alternating electrical energy from one circuit to another at constant frequency. The fundamental operation relies upon Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction and mutual induction between two magnetically coupled stationary windings. When an alternating voltage $V_1$ is supplied across the primary winding, it sets up an alternating magnetizing current $I_\mu$.

2. CORE FLUX PHENOMENOLOGY

This exciting current drives a continuous sinusoidal magnetic flux $\Phi(t) = \Phi_m \sin(\omega t)$ through the laminated silicon steel core. This magnetic flux travels around the magnetic circuit linking completely with both the primary turns $N_1$ and secondary turns $N_2$. The core has high permeability $\mu_r$ and offers very low reluctance path to the mutual magnetic lines of force.

3. EMF DERIVATION & TURNS RATIO

By rate of change of flux linkages, the instantaneous induced electromotive force in the primary is given by $e_1 = -N_1 \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -N_1 \omega \Phi_m \cos(\omega t)$. Its RMS value is:

$E_1 = 4.44 \cdot f \cdot N_1 \cdot \Phi_m$  and  $E_2 = 4.44 \cdot f \cdot N_2 \cdot \Phi_m$ Valid Derivation

Therefore taking the transformation voltage ratio: Step 3: $\frac{E_2}{E_1} = \frac{N_1}{N_2} = K$  (Hence secondary voltage scales inversed to turns ratio)
